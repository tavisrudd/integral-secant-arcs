\section{Modular stability and completeness}\label{sec:modular-stability}

Assume now that the plane is \(\PG(2,q)\), where \(q=p^e\).  Dualizing the
set of \(n\)-secants gives a point set \(\mathcal L^*\) in the dual plane.
The intersection of \(\mathcal L^*\) with the line dual to a point
\(X\) has size \(\rho_n(X)\) or \(\sigma_n(X)\), according as
\(X\in\mathcal K\) or \(X\notin\mathcal K\).  At a rational equality
parameter from Section~\ref{sec:rational-parameters}, the two limiting
intersection numbers are
\[
 \lambda=uv,\qquad a=(u+1)(v+1),\qquad a-\lambda=u+v+1.
\]
When \(u+v+1\) is a power of \(p\), these two numbers are congruent modulo
\(p\).

The two characteristic-specific arguments follow the same chain.  A bounded
first-order coefficient localizes \(T\) to within \(O(1)\) of its continuous
value, and the exact balancing loss then leaves only \(O(q)\) wrong-residue
dual lines.  Modular stability compresses those lines to \(O(1)\) repair
support; dualizing this support gives primal lines on which completeness
forces a linear external excess.

Sz\H{o}nyi and Weiner prove that, for \(q=p^e>27\) with \(e>1\), a multiset in
\(\PG(2,q)\) having \(\delta\) lines with the wrong intersection residue can,
under their stated upper bound on \(\delta\), be changed at exactly
\(\lceil\delta/(q+1)\rceil\) distinct support points into a multiset having
the prescribed residue on every line \cite[Theorem~1.2]{SWmodp}.  In our
application \(e>2\), \(\delta=O(q)\), and their threshold is
\((\lfloor\sqrt q\rfloor+1)(q+1-\lfloor\sqrt q\rfloor)\).  Thus the threshold
holds for all sufficiently large \(q\), and the number of changed support
points is bounded.  Related applications of
modular stability to arc and direction problems appear in
Csajb\'ok--Weiner~\cite{CsajbokWeiner} and
Adriaensen--Sz\H{o}nyi--Weiner~\cite{AdriaensenSzonyiWeiner}.

The same argument gives a structural statement on every
characteristic-compatible rational equality family.

\begin{proposition}[Stability near a rational equality family]
\label{prop:rational-family-stability}
\coverage{absent}
Fix \(u,v\ge1\), put \(\lambda=uv\), and suppose
\(d=u+v+1=p^j\) for some prime \(p\).  Along a family
\[
 q=p^e=dm,\qquad n=(u+1)m+1,\qquad k=udm^2+O(m),
 \qquad e\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
suppose every external point of a \((k,n)\)-arc lies on at least
\(\lambda\) \(n\)-secants.  Let \(\mathcal L^*\) be the dual point set of all
\(n\)-secants.  Then only \(O(q)\) lines of the dual plane meet
\(\mathcal L^*\) in a number of points different from \(\lambda\pmod p\).
Moreover, \(\mathcal L^*\) can be changed at \(O(1)\) distinct support points
into a multiset meeting every line in \(\lambda\pmod p\) points.
\imports{SWmodp:repair}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(k=udm^2+c_m m\), where \(c_m\) is bounded.  By
Corollary~\ref{cor:n-secant-feasibility}, the actual secant number satisfies
\(F(T)\le0\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:secant-number-near-equality} therefore gives
\[
 T=ud(v+1)m+O(1).
\]
Put \(a=(u+1)(v+1)\), so \(a-\lambda=d\equiv0\pmod p\).
The exact formulas defining \(F\), evaluated with bounded \(c_m\) and this
value of \(T\), give \(F(T)=O(m)\).  The pair identity therefore gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 -F(T)={}&
 \left(\sum_{P\in\mathcal K}\binom{\rho_n(P)}2-\Phimin(k,nT)\right)\\
 &+\left(\sum_{Q\notin\mathcal K}\binom{\sigma_n(Q)}2
              -\Phimin(b,(q+1-n)T)\right)=O(m).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Both parentheses are nonnegative.  For all sufficiently large \(m\), put
\(b_0=\lfloor nT/k\rfloor\in\{a-1,a\}\).  For every integer
\(z\notin\{b_0,b_0+1\}\),
\[
 |z-a|\le (z-b_0)(z-b_0-1).
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:balancing-loss} therefore shows that all but \(O(m)\)
internal degrees lie in the adjacent balanced pair
\(\{b_0,b_0+1\}\subseteq\{a-1,a,a+1\}\), and that
\[
 \sum_{\substack{P\in\mathcal K\\
        \rho_n(P)\notin\{b_0,b_0+1\}}}
 |\rho_n(P)-a|=O(m).
\]
Since
\[
 ak-nT=\sum_{P\in\mathcal K}(a-\rho_n(P))=O(m),
\]
and within either adjacent pair every degree unequal to \(a\) contributes
the same signed unit, the number of internal degrees unequal to \(a\) is
\(O(m)\).  Externally,
the coverage hypothesis and the incidence sum give
\[
 \sum_{Q\notin\mathcal K}(\sigma_n(Q)-\lambda)
 =(q+1-n)T-\lambda b=O(m),
\]
so only \(O(m)\) external degrees differ from \(\lambda\).  Thus only
\(O(m)=O(q)\) dual lines have the wrong intersection residue.

For all sufficiently large \(q\), this number lies below the
Sz\H{o}nyi--Weiner threshold quoted above.  Their theorem changes
\(\mathcal L^*\) at exactly \(\lceil\delta/(q+1)\rceil=O(1)\) distinct
support points to obtain the required \(\lambda\pmod p\) multiset.  We take
the support modulo \(p\), deleting any point whose correction coefficient is
zero; every listed support point therefore has nonzero coefficient modulo
\(p\).
\imports{SWmodp:repair}
\uses{lem:secant-number-near-equality,cor:n-secant-feasibility}
\end{proof}

The following observation is where completeness re-enters the modular
argument.

\begin{lemma}[Contribution of the repair support]
\label{lem:repair-support}
\coverage{absent}
Fix an ordered factorization \(\lambda=uv\) and suppose
\(d=u+v+1\) is a power of the characteristic.  Let
\[
 q=dm,\qquad n=(u+1)m+1,\qquad
 k=udm^2+cm+o(m),\qquad
 T=ud(v+1)m+h+o(1),
\]
where \(h\in\mathbb Z\).  Suppose every external point is on at least
\(\lambda\) \(n\)-secants, and suppose \(\mathcal L^*\) can be changed at
\(r\) distinct support points, with nonzero correction coefficient modulo
\(p\) at each, into a multiset meeting every line in \(\lambda\pmod p\)
points.  If \(r\) is fixed and nonzero, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:repair-support-bound}
 c\ge L(h)+\frac ru.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Dualize the \(r\) support points to \(r\) lines of the original plane.
Away from their pairwise intersections, a primal point on one support line
corresponds dually to a line containing exactly one support point.  The
correction coefficient there is nonzero modulo \(p\), so the original
\(n\)-secant multiplicity differs from \(\lambda\pmod p\).  If the point is
external, its multiplicity is at least \(\lambda+1\).  A support line contains
at most \(n\) points of \(\mathcal K\), and the union loses only
\(O(r^2)\) points at pairwise intersections.  Hence the total external excess
over multiplicity \(\lambda\) is at least
\begin{equation}\label{eq:repair-excess-lower}
 r(q+1-n)-O(r^2)=rvm-O(r^2).
\end{equation}
On the other hand, the incidence sum gives the exact first-order expression
\begin{equation}\label{eq:repair-excess-exact}
 (q+1-n)T-\lambda(q^2+q+1-k)
 =uv\bigl(c-L(h)\bigr)m+o(m).
\end{equation}
Comparing \eqref{eq:repair-excess-lower} and
\eqref{eq:repair-excess-exact} proves \eqref{eq:repair-support-bound}.
\end{proof}

There is a useful restriction when \(r=1\).  Summing the line intersections
of an exact \(\lambda\pmod p\) multiset over a pencil shows that its total
multiplicity is \(\lambda\pmod p\).  Along a characteristic-compatible
family write \(d=p^j\) and \(q=p^e=dm\).  Then \(m=p^{e-j}\) is divisible
by \(p\) once \(e>j\), so the leading term of \(T\) is divisible by \(p\).
The correction therefore has total multiplicity
\(\lambda-h\pmod p\).  Consequently, if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:support-one-obstruction}
 h\equiv\lambda\pmod p,
\end{equation}
then a nonzero correction cannot have support one.

We apply this first to ordinary completeness.  Write \(q=3m\),
\(n=2m+1\), and suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:characteristic-three-asymptotic}
 k=3m^2+cm+o(m),\qquad T=6m+h+o(1).
\end{equation}
The coverage and pair-count inequalities from Sections~\ref{sec:n-secants}
and \ref{sec:rational-parameters} become
\begin{equation}\label{eq:characteristic-three-linear-bounds}
 c+h\ge3,\qquad 5c-h\ge18.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[The uncorrected case is impossible]
\label{lem:no-uncorrected-characteristic-three}
\coverage{absent}
Let \(\mathcal K\) be a complete \((k,n)\)-arc with parameters
\eqref{eq:characteristic-three-asymptotic}, and let \(\mathcal L^*\) be the
dual point set of all its \(n\)-secants.  If \(c<4\) and every line meets
\(\mathcal L^*\) in \(1\pmod3\) points, then
the exact pair identity \eqref{eq:n-secant-pairs} cannot hold.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Near this equality parameter the balanced internal multiplicities are
\(3,4\), and the balanced external multiplicities are \(1,2\).  Let \(D\)
be the signed internal deficiency \(4k-nT\), and let \(E\) be the total
external excess above one.  The incidence sums give
\begin{align}
 D&=(4c-2h-6)m+o(m),\label{eq:D-characteristic-three}\\
 E&=(c+h-3)m+o(m).\label{eq:E-characteristic-three}
\end{align}
Completeness gives \(E\ge0\).  Moreover,
\eqref{eq:characteristic-three-linear-bounds} and \(c<4\) give
\(D\ge(30-6c)m+o(m)>0\).
Without the congruence restriction, the available pair-count slack is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-slack-characteristic-three}
 (5c-h-18)m+o(m).
\end{equation}
If every multiplicity is \(1\pmod3\), congruence-restricted integer
convexity replaces the internal adjacent values \(3,4\) by \(1,4\), raising
the minimum pair count by \(D\).  It replaces the external adjacent values
\(1,2\) by \(1,4\), raising that minimum by \(E\).  Feasibility would therefore
require
\[
 5c-h-18\ge \frac{D+E}{m}+o(1)=5c-h-9+o(1),
\]
which is impossible.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Characteristic-three modular-repair bound]
\label{prop:characteristic-three-modular-bound}
\coverage{fragment}
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), all sufficiently large powers \(q=3^e\) have
the following property: every complete \((k,2q/3+1)\)-arc in \(\PG(2,q)\)
satisfies
\[
 k\ge \frac{q^2}{3}+\left(\frac43-\varepsilon\right)q.
\]
\imports{SWmodp:repair}
\lean{characteristicThree_affine_bounds}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose the assertion fails.  For some \(\varepsilon>0\), pass to a sequence
with
\[
 k\le3m^2+(4-3\varepsilon)m.
\]
The classical incidence bound supplies a lower bound for
\(c_m=(k-3m^2)/m\), so, after passing to a subsequence, \(c_m\to c\) with
\(c\le4-3\varepsilon\).  The leading real inequality has a unique equality
point at \(T/m=6\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:secant-number-near-equality} then gives
\(T-6m=O(1)\); pass to a further subsequence on which this difference is a
fixed integer \(h\).

For the actual degree sequences, the pair identity gives the exact
decomposition
\[
 -F(T)=
 \left(\sum_{P\in\mathcal K}\binom{\rho_n(P)}2-\Phimin(k,nT)\right)
 +\left(\sum_{Q\notin\mathcal K}\binom{\sigma_n(Q)}2
              -\Phimin(b,(q+1-n)T)\right).
\]
Both parentheses are nonnegative.  Equation~\eqref{eq:pair-linear}, with
bounded \(c_m\) and fixed \(h\), shows that their sum is \(O(m)\).
Equation~\eqref{eq:balancing-loss} therefore shows that only \(O(m)\)
internal degrees lie outside \(\{3,4\}\), and only \(O(m)\) external degrees
lie outside \(\{1,2\}\).

It remains to count the wrong adjacent degrees.  The signed deficiency
\(D=4k-nT=O(m)\) equals \(\sum_{P\in\mathcal K}(4-\rho_n(P))\).
For every nonnegative integer \(z\notin\{3,4\}\),
\[
 |4-z|\le (z-3)(z-4),
\]
so the total contribution of the nonadjacent internal degrees is \(O(m)\)
by \eqref{eq:balancing-loss}.  Hence the number of internal degree-three
points is \(O(m)\).  Externally, completeness gives \(\sigma_n(Q)\ge1\), and
the excess \(E=\sum_Q(\sigma_n(Q)-1)=O(m)\) bounds the number of degree-two
points.  Thus only \(O(m)=O(q)\) dual lines fail to meet \(\mathcal L^*\) in
\(1\pmod3\) points.
Sz\H{o}nyi--Weiner's repair theorem changes
\(\mathcal L^*\) at a bounded integer number \(r\) of support points to obtain
an exact \(1\pmod3\) multiset.  Pass to a further subsequence on which \(r\)
is fixed.  Lemma~\ref{lem:no-uncorrected-characteristic-three}
excludes \(r=0\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:repair-support} gives
\[
 c\ge L(h)+r=3-h+r.
\]
By \eqref{eq:support-one-obstruction}, \(r\ge2\) when
\(h\equiv1\pmod3\), and \(r\ge1\) otherwise.  Together with the pair-count
bound \(c\ge(18+h)/5\), this yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:characteristic-three-minimum}
 c\ge
 \min_{h\in\mathbb Z}
 \max\left\{\frac{18+h}{5},3-h+r_{\min}(h)\right\}=4,
\end{equation}
where \(r_{\min}(h)=2\) for \(h\equiv1\pmod3\) and is one otherwise.  This
contradicts the chosen sequence and proves the proposition.
\imports{SWmodp:repair}
\uses{lem:repair-support,lem:no-uncorrected-characteristic-three,cor:n-secant-feasibility}
\end{proof}
