\section{Introduction}

A \((k,n)\)-arc in a projective plane is a set of \(k\) points meeting every
line in at most \(n\) points, with some line meeting it in exactly \(n\)
points.  It is complete if it is maximal under inclusion, or equivalently if
every external point lies on an \(n\)-secant.  The minimum size of a complete
\((k,n)\)-arc is denoted by \(t_n(2,q)\) in
\(\PG(2,q)\).  Lower bounds for this quantity are usually obtained by counting
\(n\)-secants or, dually, by bounding minimal multiple blocking sets; see
Alabdullah--Hirschfeld~\cite{AlabdullahHirschfeld} and
Bishnoi--Mattheus--Schillewaert~\cite{BMS}.  Recent work has also developed
algebraic and curve-based methods for proving completeness itself; see, for
example, Bastioni--Micheli~\cite{BastioniMicheli},
Korchm\'aros--Nagy--Sz\H{o}nyi~\cite{KNS}, and
Bartoli--Timpanella~\cite{BartoliTimpanella}.

The incidence estimate used in the existing lower bounds is an instance of
the standard block-design incidence inequality; we use the normalization of
Lund--Saraf~\cite{LundSaraf} and the variance formulation of
Murphy--Petridis~\cite{MurphyPetridis}.  Exact quotient equations for tactical
decompositions are classical~\cite{BekerMitchellPiper}, but the selected
family of all \(n\)-secants need not be tactical.  Ramani's recent
first-incidence identity for uniform intersecting linear hypergraphs
\cite{Ramani} provides a related degree-defect viewpoint.  Our argument uses
both point classes and the intervals determined by their exact pair-count
extrema.  Congruence and stability methods for line-intersection
distributions and small-weight line codes are developed by
Sz\H{o}nyi--Weiner and Adriaensen--Sz\H{o}nyi--Weiner
\cite{SWmodp,SWeven,AdriaensenSzonyiWeiner}.  Here those methods enter only
after the integer envelopes have bounded the number of exceptional dual
lines.

This paper keeps the \(n\)-secants as a family rather than eliminating their
number at the first counting step.  The degrees of that family on the arc and
on its complement have fixed sums, and their pair counts add to the number of
pairs of secants.  Integer convexity gives the exact extrema of each pair
count, and the two ranges must overlap.  Replacing the integer extrema by
quadratic real averages recovers the classical incidence-mixing inequality.
The pair identity and convex extrema are elementary; their simultaneous use
keeps both the integer remainders and the secant number visible.  On the
rational equality families classified below, the remainders have linear order
and the secant number localizes to a bounded interval.  Modular stability and
completeness then impose a second linear penalty.

The same integer remainder governs a different incidence structure in
\cite{RuddSecantDefects}: collisions between ordinary secants of an arc or
cap, counted through the coplanar quadruples on each secant, rather than
incidences between points and a selected family of maximal secants.  That
paper bounds complete caps in higher dimension and arcs complete outside a
prescribed set of holes; this one bounds complete \((k,n)\)-arcs in the
plane.  The counting step is shared, the degree sequences are not, and
neither result contains the other.

The first application concerns ordinary completeness in characteristic
three.

\begin{theorem}[Characteristic-three bound]\label{thm:characteristic-three}
\coverage{fragment}
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), there is \(h_0\) such that, for \(h\ge h_0\),
every complete \((k,2q/3+1)\)-arc in \(\PG(2,q)\), where \(q=3^h\), satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:characteristic-three-bound}
 k\ge \frac{q^2}{3}+\left(\frac53-\varepsilon\right)q.
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\[
 t_{2q/3+1}(2,q)\ge \frac{q^2}{3}+\frac{5q}{3}-o(q)
 \qquad (q=3^h\to\infty).
\]
\imports{SWmodp:repair,SWmodp:small-codeword}
\lean{characteristicThree_finalCoefficient}
\end{theorem}

The incidence-mixing bound gives only \(q^2/3+q+O(1)\) in this regime.  The
integer degree bounds first raise the linear coefficient to \(6/5\); a
modular repair theorem of Sz\H{o}nyi--Weiner~\cite{SWmodp} then raises it to
\(4/3\).  The second step uses more than modular stability: each point changed
in the dual repaired multiset becomes a primal line on which completeness
forces a linear amount of external secant excess.
At that center, exact moments produce a ternary line-code word of weight
\(O(q)\).  Sz\H{o}nyi--Weiner's exact small-codeword theorem represents it on
only a bounded number of lines.  Two pointwise integer-shell inequalities
force at least three generator lines and show that three lines require an
additional \(q/3-o(q)\) points.  Combining this gap with the modular-repair
bound yields the coefficient \(5/3\).
At equality in the linear term, the signed three-line cores admit a finite
structural reduction.  Concurrent generators are impossible, and the two
remaining triangular sign patterns each violate the arc cap; see
Proposition~\ref{prop:exact-five-thirds-endpoint}.  Thus the exact
\(5/3\) endpoint is absent, although this does not exclude positive
sublinear displacement.
Proposition~\ref{prop:rational-family-stability} isolates the structural part
for every characteristic-compatible rational equality family: at the leading
equality density, the full dual secant set is a bounded edit of an exact
\(\lambda\pmod p\) multiset.  This reduces, but does not solve, classification
of the geometric extremizers.

The arithmetic source of the first improvement is visible whenever the real
incidence bound is tight at a rational parameter.  Fix a coverage multiplicity
\(\lambda\), factor it as
\(\lambda=uv\), and put \(d=u+v+1\).  For \(h\in\mathbb Z\), define
\begin{align}
 c_{\mathrm{bal}}
 &=\frac{u(uv^2+4uv+2u+2v^2+4v+1)}{2uv+u+v},\label{eq:c-balanced}\\
 h_*&=-\frac{u(v+1)(u^2v+u^2-v)}{2uv+u+v},\label{eq:h-star}\\
 \mu&=\frac{2v}{(u+v)(2uv+u+v+1)},\nonumber\\
 L(h)&=d-\frac hu,\qquad
 C(h)=c_{\mathrm{bal}}+\mu(h-h_*),\nonumber\\
 c_{\mathbb Z}&=\min_{h\in\mathbb Z}\max\{L(h),C(h)\}.
 \label{eq:c-integer}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Rational equality families]\label{thm:factor-pair}
\coverage{fragment}
Let \(u,v\ge1\), \(\lambda=uv\), and \(d=u+v+1\).  Suppose that a
\((k,(u+1)m+1)\)-arc in a projective plane of order \(q=dm\) has at least
\(\lambda\) \(n\)-secants through every external point.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor-pair-bound}
 k\ge udm^2+c_{\mathbb Z}m-O_{u,v}(1).
\end{equation}
Moreover, the classical incidence-mixing bound has linear coefficient
\[
 c_{\mathrm{mix}}=\frac{u(uv+3u+2v+3)}{2u+1},
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strict-gap}
 c_{\mathrm{bal}}-c_{\mathrm{mix}}
 =\frac{u(v+1)(u^2+u+1)}{(2u+1)(2uv+u+v)}>0.
\end{equation}
Every rational equality parameter of the continuous bound whose limiting
average internal degree is an integer \(a>\lambda\) arises from exactly one
ordered factorization \(\lambda=uv\).
\imports{BMS:spectral-bound}
\lean{coefficient_difference_factorization}
\end{theorem}

Thus the strict improvement is not an isolated rounding effect.  At fixed
coverage multiplicity there is one equality family for each divisor, and every
family has a positive linear integrality penalty.  The two integers adjacent
to \(h_*\) determine \(c_{\mathbb Z}\), so the coefficient is explicit.

The same integer necessary condition holds in arbitrary symmetric designs.
In a projective plane there are two further bounds.  At most
\(\lfloor(k-1)/(n-1)\rfloor\) \(n\)-secants pass through a point of the arc,
and at most \(\lfloor k/n\rfloor\) pass through an external point.  These
geometric bounds sharpen the general condition for complete arcs.

In characteristic two the first family in which the two first-order bounds
are simultaneously tight yields a second
application.

\begin{theorem}[Characteristic-two parity bound]\label{thm:characteristic-two}
\coverage{fragment}
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), there is \(h_0\) such that, for \(h\ge h_0\),
if \(q=2^h\) and every external point of a
\((k,3q/4+1)\)-arc in \(\PG(2,q)\) lies on at least two \((3q/4+1)\)-secants,
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:characteristic-two-bound}
 k\ge \frac{q^2}{2}+\left(\frac{73}{48}-\varepsilon\right)q.
\end{equation}
\imports{SWeven:repair}
\lean{characteristicTwo_odd_affine_bounds,
characteristicTwo_even_affine_bounds}
\end{theorem}

The proof separates odd and even repair support.  The parity of the selected
secant family fixes the parity of the support, while the symmetric difference
of its generator lines supplies respectively \(q/4-O(1)\) or \(q/2-O(1)\)
external points of excess degree.

The projective-code translation is immediate.  A spanning point set in
\(\PG(r-1,q)\) gives a projective \([k,r,k-n]_q\) code.  Hyperplanes meeting
the point set in \(n\) columns correspond to minimum-weight codewords, and a
candidate extension column is obstructed by each such hyperplane through it.
The same integer degree theorem
therefore bounds nonextendibility with multiple minimum-weight witnesses.
We keep the finite-geometry language primary and record this translation
after the planar applications.

The paper is organized as follows.  Section~\ref{sec:integer-envelopes}
proves the symmetric-design inequality and identifies incidence mixing as its
real relaxation.  The next two sections specialize to \(n\)-secants and
prove Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-pair}.  The modular-stability section proves an
intermediate characteristic-three bound, the line-code section proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:characteristic-three}, and the next section proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:characteristic-two}.  The final section gives the blocking-set and
coding translations and records the exact computational checks used to guard
the finite algebra.
